# Video Hook Bank — the comeback video (and every one after)
**Built Sep 21, 2026.** Research-backed hook options matched to footage actually in hand.
Companion to `Video_Scripts.md` and `Social_Media_Strategy.md`. Supersedes the hook line in `Video_Script_Big_Reset.md` (that video posted Jul 30).

## What the research actually says

Most "viral hook formula" content online is SEO filler with no data behind it. Two findings survived filtering, and both point the same direction:

1. **"Result First" is the strongest hook type in DIY/Maker specifically** — 84 avg retention score vs 58 for the worst type in the same niche, a 26-point gap. Across all niches it ranked #1 of 8. (Brightbean, 10k-hook analysis. Proprietary scoring, so treat as directional — but it converges with everything else.)
2. **Deliver the complete hook inside 5 seconds.** Videos that did retained ~23% more viewers at the 30-second mark than videos needing 10+ seconds of setup.

Supporting, weaker: blended hooks (two types at once) beat single-type 79 vs 73. And the recurring failure mode across analyses is *setup before claim* — backstory, credentials, context, "so last video I…".

**All of this already agrees with `Social_Media_Strategy.md`:** *"0–2 sec: Visual hook. Show the most impressive thing first. NO intro, NO 'hey guys.'"* The research doesn't change the playbook, it confirms it and tells us which specific asset to lead with.

## The structural consequence — this is the important part

"Result First" in a build series that **isn't finished yet** means: open on the most finished-looking working thing you own.

That is the **breadboard fire-test clip** — a solenoid physically pressing a key, a character appearing on screen. It is the only footage in the archive where the machine does the thing.

**So it goes at 0:00, not only at 0:40.** Use it twice:

- **0:00–0:02** — the press, real sound, no music, no talking. Proof before claim.
- **0:02–0:05** — the hook line lands over it or immediately after.
- ...build content...
- **0:38–0:48** — the fuller version of the same clip as the payoff, now that the viewer knows what it cost.

Same shot, two jobs. Opening cold on a black screen or a face is spending the first two seconds on the least interesting frame you have.

## HOOK BANK

Each one is tagged with the pattern it copies and the shot it plays over. Every hook should be **spoken AND on-screen as text** — most viewers are sound-off on the first watch.

### Tier 1 — lead with these

**H1 — "This keyboard types by itself."** · *Result First* · over the fire-test press
> Then, after the beat: *"Well — one key does. So far."*

The chosen line, tightened. Present tense beats "is going to" — a promise is weaker than a claim you're about to prove, and you can make the claim honestly because the clip is playing while you say it. The walk-back in the second line is the hook's engine: it converts a flat statement into an open loop (*how far off is he?*) and it's honest, which the rest of the video has to be anyway. Blended: Result First + a soft contrarian turn.

**H2 — "That was a robot pressing that key. I need 83 more."** · *Result First + Shocking Stat* · over the fire-test press
The number does the work without jargon, and it arrives *after* the viewer has already seen one work — so 83 reads as scale, not as noise. Strongest pure-numbers option.

**H3 — "One key down. Eighty-three to go."** · *Result First* · over the press, hard cut to the full plate on the dorm desk
Shortest in the bank — lands in about 1.5 seconds, well inside the 5-second rule, and the cut to 84 solenoids IS the punchline. Best option if you want the song to drop early.

### Tier 2 — strong, different flavor

**H4 — "There's a way to do this in software in about four lines of code. I'm not doing that."** · *Contrarian Claim* (#2 overall in the research) · over the plate overhead
This is the "why" from `Social_Media_Strategy.md` weaponized as a hook instead of buried as an explainer. Pre-empts the top comment, which makes the comments section argue *with each other* instead of with you. Use when you want the thoughtful-engineering audience over the spectacle audience.

**H5 — "Every single key on this keyboard is getting its own robot."** · *Direct Promise* · over a slow push across the populated plate
No jargon, no number required, instantly legible to someone who has never seen the project. The safest cold-start hook for a brand-new viewer — and after 7 weeks off, most of your reach is brand-new viewers.

**H6 — "233 solder joints. That's one board. I need three."** · *Shocking Stat* · over the bare PCB before-shot
Sets up the timelapse better than anything else here, because it makes the viewer want to watch the work rather than skip it. Downside: opens on effort rather than result, which is exactly the trade the research penalizes. Use it as the *second* beat at 0:05, not at 0:00.

### Tier 3 — the gap, handled

**H7 — "Last time you saw this it was a pile of wires on my desk. It's now a pile of wires 900 miles away."** · *Story Setup* · over the dorm desk wide
Addresses the 7-week absence and the move in one line without apologizing. Only use if the dorm-desk frame is genuinely good. Story Setup ranked #3 of 8 — respectable, but it spends the opening on you instead of the machine.

**H8 — "I moved to college and the first thing I unpacked was this."** · *Story Setup* · dorm desk, plate revealed
The salvageable version of the line you rejected. Works because the reveal carries it; the words alone are nothing. Weaker than Tier 1 for reach, better than Tier 1 for the people already following.

### Rejected — do not use

- **"I brought 84 solenoids to college."** Jargon in the first 3 seconds. "Solenoid" means nothing to a cold viewer, so "84" has no weight, and the sentence is about logistics rather than the machine.
- **"How do you make a keyboard type by itself?"** Question hooks ranked mid-pack (#5) and this one has no stakes — it invites the viewer to answer "you don't" and scroll.
- **Anything opening with "Part 6" / "Day 53" / "so in my last video."** Setup before claim, the single most-cited failure across every analysis. New viewers read it as "you've missed something" and leave.
- **"Everyone else brought a mini fridge."** Funny, but it's Identity Callout (#6) and it makes the video about dorm life instead of the build.

## Rules that apply regardless of which hook wins

1. **Hook complete by 0:05.** No exceptions. The 23% retention delta is the largest single number in this research.
2. **Real sound on the key press. Always.** Trending audio over the one moment a solenoid fires is the worst available edit — `Social_Media_Strategy.md` §The Audio Advantage is right that the sound is the competitive moat. Duck the music to zero for those 2 seconds and bring it back.
3. **Hook as on-screen text too**, not just VO.
4. **The number is 84 solenoids**, not 88. (88 = channel count including 4 spares.)
5. **Series numbering goes in the caption and title, never the first 3 seconds.**
6. **One promise per video, paid inside the same video.** The Jul 30 post ended on a 6-day countdown that went 53 days — don't write another closer you can't cash.

## Recommendation

**H1, with the fire-test clip cold-opening underneath it.**

It's the line you already picked, it's the top-performing pattern in your exact niche, the walk-back makes it honest and opens a loop, and it needs zero context from the previous six videos — which matters more than usual, because after seven weeks off most of the audience seeing this will have never seen the project before.
